Who's on the mat with you

Two teachers, taught differently on purpose

We kept Groundwork to two instructors so classes stay small enough to notice your alignment without a microphone. Each teaches their own rhythm.

Mornings & strength-building flow

Devdutt Sharma

Devdutt teaches the early classes — slow to start, built to build heat. He trained in vinyasa and spends most of a session on the small joints people forget: wrists, ankles, the base of the neck.

"Mobility first. Strength has somewhere to stand once it's there."

Evenings & balance-focused practice

Ishika Agrawal

Ishika runs the evening sessions, built around balance work and long holds. She corrects by demonstration more than instruction, and ends every class with a full five minutes of stillness — no exceptions.

"If you can hold tree pose without a wobble, you weren't trying hard enough."

Inside a session

Every class moves through the same three stretches of time

The postures change week to week. This shape doesn't.

Minutes 0–10

Arrive

Seated breathing and slow joint circles. No posture is attempted cold.

Minutes 10–45

Move

The main flow — standing sequences, balance holds, and floor work matched to that day's focus.

Minutes 45–55

Rest

Savasana, lights down. This part is not optional, even on the busy days.
